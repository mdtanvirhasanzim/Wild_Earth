Chasing the Last Light.

A field note

There is a particular kind of darkness that exists before sunrise. It is not quite night anymore, but the landscape has not yet decided what it will become.

For landscape photography, that uncertainty is often the most interesting part. I arrive early, leave the camera in the bag, and spend the first few minutes simply looking. The horizon changes before the subject does.

Last Hope
Peace In Last Light
Last time to get up

The temptation is to make the photograph immediately. Instead, I wait. Clouds move. Shadows separate. A ridge that looked flat becomes layered. The light finds a single patch of rock and suddenly the composition is there.

Patience is not passive. It is a way of participating in a place. You become aware of wind direction, birds crossing the frame, changing color and the exact moment when the scene stops being ordinary.

That is what I want from these photographs: not proof that I visited somewhere beautiful, but a small record of what it felt like to stand there and pay attention.
